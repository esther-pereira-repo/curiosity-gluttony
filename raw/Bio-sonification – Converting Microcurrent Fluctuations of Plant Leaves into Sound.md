### I. INTRODUCTION

The field of plant physiology and bioacoustics has recently witnessed a paradigm shift with the discovery that plants emit audible sounds in response to stressors. Traditionally, plant communication has been predominantly understood through chemical signals, facilitating interactions with their environment and neighbouring organisms \[1\]. However, a groundbreaking study by Khait et al. has unveiled a hitherto hidden dimension of plant behaviour: the generation of airborne sounds under varying stress conditions \[2\]. This revelation challenges established notions of plant-environment interactions, paving the way for a novel realm of inquiry termed 'plant bio-sonification' \[3\].
