**Norah Mary Vincent** (September 20, 1968 – July 6, 2022) was an American writer. She was a weekly columnist for the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")* and a quarterly columnist on politics and culture for the national [gay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT "LGBT") news magazine *[The Advocate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Advocate_\(LGBT_magazine\) "The Advocate (LGBT magazine)")*. She was a columnist for *[The Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Village_Voice "The Village Voice")* and [Salon.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon.com "Salon.com"). Her writing appeared in *[The New Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Republic "The New Republic")*, *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")*,[^1] *[New York Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Post "New York Post")*, *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")* and other periodicals.[^2] She gained particular attention in 2006 for her book *[Self-Made Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-Made_Man_\(book\) "Self-Made Man (book)")*, detailing her experiences when she lived as a man for eighteen months.

## Early life

Norah Mary Vincent was born in [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit "Detroit"), [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan "Michigan"), and grew up both there and in [London Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Township,_Michigan "London Township, Michigan") where her father was employed as a lawyer for the [Ford Motor Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company "Ford Motor Company").[^3] She attended [Williams College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_College "Williams College"), where she graduated with a BA in philosophy in 1990, before undertaking graduate studies at [Boston College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_College "Boston College").[^2] [^3] She also worked as an editor for [Free Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Press_\(publisher\) "Free Press (publisher)").[^3]

## Career

### Self-Made Man

Vincent's book *[Self-Made Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-Made_Man_\(book\) "Self-Made Man (book)")* (2006) retells an eighteen-month experiment in the early 2000s in which she [disguised herself as a man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-dressing "Cross-dressing").[^1] [^4] This was compared to previous [undercover journalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undercover_journalism "Undercover journalism") such as *[Black Like Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Like_Me "Black Like Me")*.[^3] Vincent was interviewed by [Juju Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juju_Chang "Juju Chang") on the [ABC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_News_\(United_States\) "ABC News (United States)") program [*20/20*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20/20_\(American_TV_program\) "20/20 (American TV program)") [^5] and talked about the experience in *[HARDtalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HARDtalk "HARDtalk")* extra on [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") on April 21, 2006, where she described her experiences in male-male and male-female relationships. She joined an all-male [bowling club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_club "Bowling club"),[^1] joined a men's [therapy group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Therapy_group "Therapy group"), went to a [strip club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strip_club "Strip club"),[^1] dated women, and used her knowledge as a lapsed [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic "Catholic") [^1] [^5] [^6] to visit monks in a [monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey "Abbey").[^7]

Vincent wrote that the only time she has ever been considered excessively feminine was during her stint as a man. Her [alter ego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alter_ego "Alter ego"), Ned, was assumed to be gay on several occasions. Features which had been perceived as [butch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butch_and_femme "Butch and femme") when she presented as a woman were perceived as oddly effeminate when she presented as a man. Vincent asserted that, since the experiment, she had more fully realized the benefits of being female and the disadvantages of being male, stating, "I really like being a woman.... I like it more now because I think it's more of a privilege." [^5]

Vincent also stated that she had gained more sympathy and understanding for men and the male condition: "Men are suffering. They have different problems than women have but they don't have it better. They need our sympathy, they need our love, and they need each other more than anything else. They need to be together." [^5]

### Voluntary Madness

Vincent's book *Voluntary Madness* (2008) relates her experiences as an inpatient in three institutions for mentally ill patients: "a ward in a public city hospital, a private Midwestern institution, and a pricey New Age clinic." [^8] She criticized doctors who she claimed were unapproachable, noting that too many relied on drugs as therapy, while others addressed only symptoms instead of their underlying causes.[^9]

Vincent's book also addresses the question of [pseudopatients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudopatient "Pseudopatient") and those who remained ill because of their lack of willingness to cooperate in their therapy.[^1] [^10]

### Later work

Vincent later wrote two novels: *Thy Neighbor* (2012), described by *The New York Times* as "a dark, comic thriller", and *Adeline* (2015), which imagines the life of [Virginia Woolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Woolf "Virginia Woolf") from when she wrote *[To the Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_the_Lighthouse "To the Lighthouse")* until Woolf's suicide in 1941.[^3]

## Personal life, views, and death

Vincent, who identified as a lesbian, was briefly married to Kristen Erickson, but soon divorced.[^3]

Vincent was described as a [libertarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarianism_in_the_United_States "Libertarianism in the United States") who was critical of [postmodernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmodern_philosophy "Postmodern philosophy") and [multiculturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism "Multiculturalism").[^3] She did not believe that transgender people were part of the gender they identified as.[^3] In an article for *The Village Voice*, she wrote: "\[Transsexuality\] signifies the death of the self, the soul, that good old-fashioned indubitable 'I' so beloved of [Descartes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Descartes "René Descartes"), whose great adage ' [I think, therefore I am](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cogito,_ergo_sum "Cogito, ergo sum") ' has become an ontological joke on the order of 'I tinker, and there I am.'" [^11]

In *Voluntary Madness*, Vincent details her decade-long history with treatment-resistant depression, saying: "...my brain was never quite the same after I zapped it with that first course of [SSRIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selective_serotonin_reuptake_inhibitor "Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor")." [^12] Due to her experience as a man during the making of *Self-Made Man* she ultimately had a depressive breakdown, leading Vincent to admit herself to a locked psychiatric facility, stating it was the high price she paid for "the burden of deception" of a separate identity and for trying to hold two gender identities in her mind.[^13] [^14]

Vincent died via [assisted suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_suicide "Assisted suicide") at a clinic in Switzerland on July 6, 2022, aged 53. Her death was reported in August 2022.[^3]
