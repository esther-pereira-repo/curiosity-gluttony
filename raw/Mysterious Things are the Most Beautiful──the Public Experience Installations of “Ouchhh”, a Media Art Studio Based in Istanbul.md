“Ouchhh” is a cutting-edge, media art studio based in Istanbul Turkey that has attracted attention from around the world, that has released a series of works in collaboration with NASA, CERN, and others. Its AI-based works, developed from the concept that "the roots of art and science are mysterious”, have been exhibited in major cities around the world. This is WIRED Japan’s interview with Ferdi Alici, the founder of Ouchhh, about its activities.

## An art studio based in Istanbul and started 10 years ago

At the “Future and Art Exhibition” held at the Mori Art Museum in 2020, the image data of the Göbekli Tepe, a Turkish archaeological site dating back to 9,600-7,000 B.C., was used to train GAN and AI algorithms in order to create “DATAMONOLITH”, which was shown on a monolithic screen.

This experimental fusion of ancient data and current AI as public art was presented by Ouchhh, an art studio based in Istanbul, Turkey.

The concept behind Ouchhh’s activities is, “The most beautiful thing we can experience is the mysterious”, a famous phrase of physicist Albert Einstein’s. We interviewed Ferdi Arissi, media artist and founder of Ouchhh Studio, which celebrated its 10th anniversary in 2020, about its work.

**──Ouchhh is celebrating its 10th anniversary. How did it start?**

The initial members of Ouchhh all came from the Visual Communication Design Faculty at the same university in Istanbul, and we started the studio under the influence of our university mentor. Over the past 10 years, the studio has grown, and the scale of our work has grown as well.

The team currently consists of 25 people, including engineers, academics, creative coders, designers, media artists, motion graphic designers, and other talented individuals from a variety of fields. We work together with the shared vision that “knowledge” is the key to creating a magnificent public experience.

**──The name of the studio is Ouchhh. Is that from the English word “Ouch!”?**

We didn’t want a word that had too much meaning. We were looking for a universal name that everyone would recognize to some extent, but that would make people wonder, “What exactly does that mean?”. “Ouch!” was an exclamation in English and, of course, we had heard of it, but we didn’t know what it really meant, so we decided to use it.

**──You are presenting large-scale installations in major cities around the world.**

Yes. Our base is in Istanbul, but we have shown our work in Los Angeles, Berlin, Vienna, Seoul, Barcelona, and elsewhere.

We use AI data to integrate all art, science, and technology disciplines, and we focus on creating immersive experiences that allow people to discover new technological models and to visualize future perspectives. We want to break down existing barriers and go beyond what seems to be the limits of architecture, science, art, media art, technology, and AI.

**──How do you feel about the evolution of technology in the 10 years since the studio was founded?**

I think it can be analyzed from two viewpoints: creativity and business.

From a creative standpoint, I can say that the technological progress of the past 10 years has been about the equivalent of 100 years of regular development. More and more devices, software, AI, and technology are evolving every day, and I believe that we have been successfully incorporating them into our art.

On the other hand, 10 years ago, creating works on computers was still very much of ‘a geek thing’, and it wasn’t much of a field of business. But now, activities like ours have become very large-scale business, and the revenue has expanded to much more than it was back then.

**──When you say ‘large scale’, many Parisians must have been exposed to Ouchhh’s work through “POETIC AI”, which was exhibited in Paris in 2018, with an audience of one million people.**

“POETIC AI” was exhibited for 9 months, and 1 million people bought tickets and visited the venue. This was the world’s largest exhibition using AI, with 146 projectors in a hall of approximately 3,500 square meters.

At that point in 2018, POETIC AI was a game-changing exhibition for the field of AI-based new media as it was able to host an installation of this scale.

**──What kind of system was used for the exhibit?**

The art was based on the machine-learning of books, research papers, etc. written by about 20 million scientists, using an AI algorithm created by Python coding as well as TensorFlow which enabled AI to read 20 million lines of text, rendering new sentences.

The lines projected by the projectors were formed from dots, each of which was a word extracted from 20 million pieces of data created by AI. To visualize this information, AI projected patterns that made the words sparkle like constellations and stars.

This exhibit asked the question, “Could AI algorithms produce poetic breakdown from an accumulation of data?”.

**──By the way, did you intend to create works using such cutting-edge technology from the very beginning of the studio?**

If you are asking me if we could have technically done the same thing 10 years earlier, I can definitely say that we could not. In addition to the fact that “what can be done” has evolved so much in the past 10 years, while we were actually creating these works, at first we did not think that AI would be able to create something meaningful, and they were just intended to be random arrangements of sentences, words, etc. But now, AI can create meaningful sentences, and we have been amazed at the speed of its evolution.

**──What have been some of the difficulties in creating such works?**

For example, in 1998, we initiated a project called “SAY SUPERSTRINGS” to visualize the brain waves of musicians while they were playing. But, at first, we had a hard time because all the devices designed to measure brain waves were too expensive and were totally unavailable.

Only later was a low cost device that could measure brain waves developed in the U.S., and we were finally able to carry out this project.

The difficulty in creating works using technology is that, even though you have ideas, you sometimes cannot find appropriate devices that have the required performance or that meet your financial requirements.

## Ouchhh’s secret message

**──What kind of message does Ouchhh incorporate into each work?**

When we start a project, we set a big question. We don’t want to present a message that is too easy to understand and, instead, we want people who come into contact with the work to look for the hidden message in the art and to feel it deeply.

We would like to create the experience of searching for those messages, and to enrich people’s daily lives by inspiring them with our art.

**──DATAMONOLITH, exhibited at the Mori Art Museum in 2019, was a novel exhibit that combined the world’s oldest ancient messages with the latest AI data.**

For the Mori Art Museum exhibit, we collected data from Göbekli Tepe, the world’s oldest archaeological site, which existed about 7,000 years before the Stone Age, and created software to create visuals based on the topographical data and other information about the site. And, we used GAN to train about 3 billion pixels.

We trained AI with that data and developed a data sculpture to create a digital “monolith”. Thousands of years ago, people were drawing on that stone monolith and, thousands of years later, an algorithm that learned from those drawings drew new pictures on a digital monolith. With this work, we could experience the hidden messages of ancient people and the magnificent connections between different times and space.

## AI is like a baby

**──Do you find that you are inspired by what AI creates?**

Every time we work with AI, we are excited to see what will be created.

With AI, you have to teach the computer to do things a certain way and to create certain things using a certain kind of algorithm. Only by teaching it will there be some kind of output.

So, if you teach it the same data, you will always get the same pattern. So, that means that, when you get something that is not what you expected, you have to change the way that you input the data. So, that means that, in order to get something that is not what you expected, you have to change the way that you input the data.

**──Indeed, if the AI does something other than what you taught it to do, that would be an error. In terms of creating a work of art, do you think it is necessary to have AI read in a lot of varied data?**

That’s right. For example, “POETIC AI” is based on books, but if you make AI do similar things, it will only produce similar things.

AI is like a baby, so to speak, so we have to feed it properly.

When you want AI to create something that is “beyond your expectations”, you have to feed it different things. This is one of the most enjoyable parts of creating things using AI.

**──Thank you very much. Lastly, please tell us what kind of work you plan to create in the future.**

I was influenced by the mysterious books that I read and movies that I watched as a child in the 80’s and 90’s, and my favorite was the science fiction TV series and movie called [Stargate](https://amzn.to/3rGqJUk).

For our next work, we are going to use data about black holes as inspiration from “Stargate,” which was about the unknown world beyond the portal, and we are going to try to convey the message that we can find the “mysterious” beyond the portal. We always want to discover other “hybrid” dimensions, and that is why we want to emphasize “mystery” in our work.
