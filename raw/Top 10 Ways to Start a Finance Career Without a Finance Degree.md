### Key Takeaways

- You can succeed in finance without an MBA or a finance major
- Social media is popular for promoting financial ideas.
- Only 45% of finance interns secure a job offer.

A finance or business degree is a prerequisite for most jobs in the [financial industry](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/f/financial_sector.asp), but what if you don’t possess one and want to work in this field? While it is more difficult for someone without a finance degree to secure a job in finance, there’s still hope.

Every employer wants smart, committed, and motivated employees who can do the job well. A finance degree will impart skills such as [financial modeling](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/f/financialmodeling.asp) and [analysis](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/f/financial-analysis.asp), but may not do much to provide other skills required for success in almost any profession, such as communication, problem solving, and time management.

The following are 10 ways to demonstrate to potential employers that you possess the skills they desire in an employee, as well as the passion necessary for a successful career in finance.

We will rate each of these by the degree of difficulty to achieve (for example, signing up for a financial course is easier than obtaining an internship), as well as the positive impact it may have on your objective of embarking on a career in the finance industry.

## Master Financial Terminology

- **Difficulty**: Low
- **Impact**: Low

If you’re interested in a career in finance, there’s no excuse for not [knowing Wall Street lingo](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/basics/09/talk-like-an-investor.asp). If you don’t know the difference between [dilution](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/d/dilution.asp) and [dividend](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/d/dividend.asp), or between [NPV](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/n/npv.asp) and [DCF](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/d/dcf.asp), consider learning financial terms and concepts by browsing the extensive [dictionary of terms](https://www.investopedia.com/financial-term-dictionary-4769738) at sites like Investopedia or by reading *The Wall Street Journal.*

Not knowing the financial language may make it almost impossible to pass the preliminary interview stage for a nonfinance graduate. An interviewer will generally assume that an applicant for a finance position is knowledgeable about finance, regardless of their educational background.

## Enhance Your Educational Background

- **Difficulty**: Low to moderate
- **Impact**: High

What if you graduated with a degree in a subject other than finance? You can always redress the situation by [taking relevant courses with an emphasis on finance](https://www.investopedia.com/warren-buffett-education-advice-11692678) or business at the undergraduate or postgraduate level.

At the undergraduate level, courses in [economics](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/e/economics.asp), [accounting](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/a/accounting.asp), or [financial analysis](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/f/financial-analysis.asp) are great options. Many postgraduates go for a [Master of Business Administration (MBA)](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/m/mba.asp) since its substantial finance component serves to level the playing field between finance and nonfinance graduates.

If the [MBA’s stiff cost](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/professionaleducation/09/mba-real-costs.asp) is a deterrent, other options such as enrolling in the [chartered financial analyst (CFA)](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/c/cfa.asp) program are certainly worth exploring. The CFA is awarded after passing a series of three rigorous exams that test a graduate level of financial theory and practical knowledge on one’s own time.1

## Join a Financial Boot Camp

- **Difficulty**: Moderate
- **Impact**: Moderate

Intensive courses by firms like Wall Street Prep and Training the Street can teach you valuable skills that are essential for a career in finance, such as advanced spreadsheet techniques and [financial modeling](https://www.investopedia.com/best-financial-modeling-courses-11745753).

These crash courses are quite expensive, typically a few thousand dollars, but have the advantage of not requiring a long-term time commitment since they are typically conducted over a few days. One drawback is that, due to these programs’ intensity, you may need to be already familiar with basic financial concepts to derive the maximum benefit.

## Broaden Your Financial Knowledge

- **Difficulty**: Moderate
- **Impact:** High

Relevant knowledge is not only obtained through a college degree. There are plenty of resources available, either through your local library or online, to deepen your financial knowledge. These resources may be free or available on a paid basis from course providers.

### Important

Being [self-taught in a difficult field like finance](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/basics/11/become-self-taught-finance-expert.asp) demonstrates a number of desirable attributes to an employer, such as initiative, passion, and drive.

## Practice With a Trading Simulator

- **Difficulty**: Moderate
- **Impact**: Low

A number of websites—including Investopedia—have [trading simulators](https://www.investopedia.com/simulator/) that can be used to construct mock [portfolios](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/p/portfolio.asp). Using a trading simulator will force you to track the [markets](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/s/stockmarket.asp) and keep abreast of market developments. This is a great way to impress a potential employer with your trading prowess, or at least your market knowledge, with very little investment on your part aside from a time commitment.

## Undertake Financial Industry Courses

- **Difficulty**: High
- **Impact**: High

Completing a relevant industry licensing course, such as that offered by the Canadian Securities Institute, not only demonstrates your commitment to a career in finance but also gives you an edge on the competition in terms of job readiness.2

This option may not be available in all jurisdictions, of course; for instance, in the United States, one has to be sponsored by a [member firm](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/m/memberfirm.asp) or a [self-regulatory organization](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/s/sro.asp) to take most licensing exams.34

However, the [Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA)](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/f/finra.asp) has created a new test called the [Securities Industry Essentials (SIE) Exam](https://www.investopedia.com/securities-industry-essentials-sie-exam-overview-4692828), which can be taken without sponsorship.5

## Start and Maintain a Finance Blog

- **Difficulty**: High
- **Impact:** Moderate

Starting and [maintaining a financial blog](https://web.archive.org/web/20210517111412/https://www.investopedia.com/terms/f/financialblog.asp) is a great way to communicate your investment ideas to the world. It is an opportunity to convey to a potential employer a favorable impression of your diverse skill set, including financial acumen, communication skills, and technological dexterity. This mode of self-marketing is most suitable for those who already possess a measure of these skills.

### Fast Fact

Increasingly, individuals looking to promote financial concepts and investment ideas are using social media platforms like Instagram, X (formerly Twitter), and TikTok to become “finfluencers.”

## Connect With a Finance Mentor

- **Difficulty**: High
- **Impact**: Moderate

[Linking up with a mentor](https://www.investopedia.com/the-value-of-a-wealth-management-mentor-11747078) is another way of jump-starting a [financial career](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/financial-careers/08/financial-career-options-professionals.asp). A mentor can be anyone in a position of influence who thinks highly of your capabilities and is willing to help you achieve your goals. Possible mentors include your favorite college professor, a family friend or relation with a successful career in finance, or someone you know in a professional capacity, such as a supervisor during a previous internship. Don’t hesitate to approach a contact who you think could help you in your job search.

## Secure a Finance Internship

- **Difficulty**: Very high
- **Impact**: Very high

[Scoring a summer internship](https://www.investopedia.com/how-to-turn-your-internship-into-a-permanent-job-11731258) still remains one of the best ways to lock in a prestigious full-time job in finance, as many Wall Street firms pick their new hires from the ranks of their summer interns. An estimated 55% of MBA students go to work for their summer employer after graduation.6

Since [obtaining a paid internship](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/younginvestors/08/land-that-internship.asp) in finance is likely to be very difficult for a nonfinance graduate, one must consider other options, such as an [unpaid internship](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/economics/12/impact-of-unpaid-internships.asp) or volunteer work with a broker. The [opportunity cost](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/o/opportunitycost.asp) that arises from doing unpaid internships or volunteer work may be offset in due course by the higher [earning potential](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/e/earning-potential.asp) of a finance career.

## Gain Entry Into the Finance Industry

- **Difficulty**: Very high
- **Impact:** Very high

Target human resources (HR) departments for resumes, expand your job search to other locations, and use your network to check for openings—in short, do everything you can to get your foot in the door of a [financial institution](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/f/financialinstitution.asp). Scoring an [entry-level position with a financial company](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/financialcareers/11/beginner-careers-in-finance.asp), even in a nonfinance role, may open doors to other career paths in finance down the line.

## Can You Earn a CFA Charter without Holding a College Degree?

No. The CFA Institute requires that charter holders have at least an accredited bachelor’s degree (in any field). However, you can apply as a candidate and begin the CFA program when you are still an undergraduate student, provided that you will successfully graduate within 11 months.1

## How Many Banking Interns End Up Getting Hired?

According to industry research, roughly 45% of finance interns who fulfill all of their requisite duties will be offered a job.7

That may seem like a favorable statistic, but keep in mind that just landing an (often unpaid) internship at a financial firm is highly competitive. In 2024, investment bank [Goldman Sachs](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/markets/041315/how-goldman-sachs-makes-its-money.asp) had a record 315,000 people apply for student internships globally. Only around 2,600 (0.9%) were accepted.8

## The Bottom Line

Some nonfinance degrees are certainly in demand on Wall Street for specific tasks, including:

- Physics and mathematics for structured products, derivatives, and quantitative trading
- Information technology (IT) for algorithmic trading and platform development
- Engineering, mining, and sciences for sector-specific research analysis and investment banking

However, for the vast majority of nonfinance degree holders, securing a job in finance is likely to pose a significant challenge. This is more so because thousands of positions were eliminated by banks and financial institutions in the aftermath of the 2008 [global recession](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/g/global-recession.asp).9

Using a combination of the tips discussed above should enable a nonfinance graduate, such as someone with a degree in information technology, mathematics, or platform development, to substantially improve their chances of launching a career in finance.
