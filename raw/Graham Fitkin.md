**Graham Fitkin** (born 19 April 1963) is a British [composer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composer "Composer"), [pianist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano "Piano") and [conductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conducting "Conducting"). His compositions fall broadly into the [minimalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimalist_music "Minimalist music") and [postminimalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postminimalism "Postminimalism") genres. Described by *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent "The Independent")* in 1998 as "one of the most important of our younger composers",[^1] he is particularly known for his works for solo and multiple pianos, as well as for music accompanying dance.

## Biography

Fitkin was born at [Crows-an-Wra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crows-an-Wra "Crows-an-Wra") in west [Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall "Cornwall") on 19 April 1963.[^2] [^3] His mother, a [piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano "Piano") teacher, encouraged his early studies on that instrument.[^4] He participated in numerous local ensembles during his childhood, and recalls starting to compose at the piano aged around 8.[^4] In 1981–4, he attended the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham "University of Nottingham"), where he studied with composer [Nigel Osborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Osborne "Nigel Osborne"), among others.[^5] He later went to the Netherlands to study with the [minimalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimalist "Minimalist") composer and pianist [Louis Andriessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Andriessen "Louis Andriessen") at the [Royal Conservatory of The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Conservatory_of_The_Hague "Royal Conservatory of The Hague").[^2] [^3] In 1987, he moved to London.[^2]

Fitkin returned to Cornwall in 1991 and, as of 2010, lives in [Treen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treen_\(St_Levan\) "Treen (St Levan)").[^2] [^6] His partner is harpist [Ruth Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ruth_Wall&action=edit&redlink=1 "Ruth Wall (page does not exist)"), with whom he collaborates in Fitkin Wall.[^6] [^7]

## Music

Fitkin's work is broadly classified as [minimalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimalist_music "Minimalist music") and [postminimalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postminimalism "Postminimalism").[^1] [^2] [^8] His works are [tonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonality "Tonality") and frequently complex. Much of his writing is for the piano, including solo and multiple player works.[^2] [^9] Fitkin lists his early classical influences as [Igor Stravinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Stravinsky "Igor Stravinsky"), [Anton Webern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Webern "Anton Webern"), [Pierre Boulez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Boulez "Pierre Boulez") and the American minimalist [Steve Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Reich "Steve Reich"), and also acknowledges a broad range of influences outside the field of classical music, from [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz") musicians [Keith Jarrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Jarrett "Keith Jarrett"), [Muggsy Spanier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muggsy_Spanier "Muggsy Spanier") and [Miles Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Davis "Miles Davis"), and popular singer [Frank Sinatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Sinatra "Frank Sinatra"), to modern pop groups such as [The Smiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Smiths "The Smiths"), [Wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wire_\(band\) "Wire (band)") and the [Pet Shop Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet_Shop_Boys "Pet Shop Boys").[^4] Subsequent influences include [Louis Andriessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Andriessen "Louis Andriessen"), [Gavin Bryars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavin_Bryars "Gavin Bryars") and [Laurence Crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Crane "Laurence Crane").[^2] [^3] [^4]

Fitkin's earliest compositions were for piano, including *From Yellow to Yellow* and *The Cone Gatherers*.[^2] The Nanquidno group, which he co-founded in 1985, consisted of four pianists using two keyboards.[^2] [^10] Several of his early works, including *Log*, *Line* and *Loud*, were composed for the six-piano ensemble, [Piano Circus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pianocircus "Pianocircus").[^2] He has also written several works for pianist [Kathryn Stott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathryn_Stott "Kathryn Stott"), including *Circuit* for two pianos and orchestra, which was composed for Stott and [Noriko Ogawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noriko_Ogawa_\(pianist\) "Noriko Ogawa (pianist)") in 2002 to a commission from the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC").[^9] [^10]

The success of his early compositions for piano, particularly *The Cone Gatherers*, led to Fitkin being commissioned to write his first ensemble work, *Cud*, for jazz orchestra.[^4] *Cud* and Fitkin's other early ensemble works including *Hook* and *Stub* often make use of electronic instruments and percussion, and are influenced by jazz and [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music").[^3] [^11] A more recent work for electronic instruments is the album *Kaplan*, which was inspired by the character George Kaplan from [Alfred Hitchcock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Hitchcock "Alfred Hitchcock") 's film, *[North by Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_by_Northwest "North by Northwest")*.[^12] In 1994–96, Fitkin was the composer-in-residence at the [Royal Liverpool Philharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Liverpool_Philharmonic "Royal Liverpool Philharmonic"), and during the mid-to-late 1990s he composed twelve orchestral pieces including a clarinet concerto.[^2] [^3] He has composed several works for [musical theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_theatre "Musical theatre"), including the short [opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera "Opera") *Ghosts*, and has also written or adapted several pieces for [contemporary dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_dance "Contemporary dance"), including *Huoah*.[^2] [^13]

Recent projects include *Still Warm*, a work for multiple [harps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harp "Harp"), which was composed for the [Eden Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Project "Eden Project") in 2006.[^14] The sextet *Sinew*, written for the [Fibonacci Sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_Sequence_\(sextet\) "Fibonacci Sequence (sextet)"), was first performed in 2009.[^15] For [Yo Yo Ma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yo_Yo_Ma "Yo Yo Ma"), he has written a cello concerto which was premiered at the 2011 [BBC Proms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Proms "The Proms"), as well as a work for cello and piano, titled *L*, composed for the performer's fiftieth birthday (2005).[^16] A BBC commission for orchestra and chorus, titled *PK*, was also premiered at the Proms in 2010.[^17] In a recent project called *Fitkin*, a group comprising 9 virtuoso musicians has been touring the UK since early 2010.[^18] In December 2010, it was announced that Fitkin had been selected as one of twenty composers to participate in the New Music 20x12 project as part of the [London 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_2012 "London 2012") Cultural Olympiad. Fitkin will compose a new work for the [London Chamber Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Chamber_Orchestra "London Chamber Orchestra") to be premiered in 2012.[^19]

In 1996, he formed the Graham Fitkin Group.[^2] His work has been released by [Decca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decca_Records "Decca Records") 's [Argo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argo_Records_\(UK\) "Argo Records (UK)") label,[^20] [Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctuary_Records_Group "Sanctuary Records Group") 's Black Box label, [BIS Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BIS_Records "BIS Records") and [Factory Classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factory_Records "Factory Records"). He founded a personal label, GFR, to release *Still Warm*.[^14]

## Awards

In 1994, Fitkin won the International Grand Prix Music for Dance Video Award.[^10] He has twice won British Composer Awards: in 2009, *Reel* won the Stage Works category;[^21] in 2011, *PK* won the Outreach category.[^22]
