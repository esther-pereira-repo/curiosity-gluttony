**Syed Hussain Sibt-e-Asghar Naqvi [^1]** (14 December 1931 – 8 November 2002), commonly known by his [pen name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takhallus "Takhallus") **Jaun Elia**,[^2] was a [Pakistani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani "Pakistani") [poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poet "Poet").[^3] [^4]

One of the most prominent modern [Urdu poets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_poets "Urdu poets") of *[ghazals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazal "Ghazal")* (amatory poems), popular for his unconventional ways, he "acquired knowledge of [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy "Philosophy"), [logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic "Logic"), [Islamic history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_history "Islamic history"), the Muslim [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi "Sufi") tradition, Muslim religious sciences, [Western literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_literature "Western literature"), and [Kabbala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabbalah "Kabbalah") ".[^5]

He was fluent in [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu "Urdu"), [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic "Arabic"), [Sindhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_language "Sindhi language"), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language") and [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farsi "Farsi").[^3] [^5] Elia was also the younger brother of poet [Rais Amrohvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rais_Amrohvi "Rais Amrohvi").

## Early life and family

Jaun Elia was born on 14 December 1931 in [Amroha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amroha "Amroha"), UP, [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India "British India") into an educated [Shia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islam "Shia Islam") family. He was the youngest child of Allama Shafiq Hassan Elia.[^3] [^6] [^7] His father was a scholar of literature and astronomy well-versed in the Arabic, English and Persian languages, and who corresponded with leading intellectuals like [Bertrand Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand_Russell "Bertrand Russell").[^8] Jaun Elia was the youngest of his siblings: his brother [Rais Amrohvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rais_Amrohvi "Rais Amrohvi") was a poet and psychoanalyst while another brother, Syed Mohammed Taqi, was a philosopher and a translator who had translated [Karl Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx "Karl Marx") 's *[Das Kapital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Kapital "Das Kapital")*.[^9] Indian film director [Kamal Amrohi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamal_Amrohi "Kamal Amrohi") was his first cousin.[^10] Another relative in Pakistan is actor [Munawar Saeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munawar_Saeed "Munawar Saeed"), famous for his roles as a villain.[^11]

Described as a child prodigy, Jaun was initially educated at the Syed-ul-Madaris in Amroha, a [madrasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasa "Madrasa") affiliated with the [Darul Uloom Deoband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darul_Uloom_Deoband "Darul Uloom Deoband").[^12]

## Marriage and Children

He married writer [Zahida Hina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahida_Hina "Zahida Hina") in the year 1970. He met her during the publication of an urdu language magazine *Insha* and ended up falling in love with her.[^3] [^13] They got separated in 1984 and later divorced due to differences in their temperament.[^3]

They have three children, Zeryoun Elia, Fainnana and Sohaina Elia [^14]

## Writing career

He began writing poetry when he was 8 but published his first collection, *Shayad*, when he was 60.[^15]

## Political views

### Partition and migration to Pakistan

Being a communist, Elia [opposed the partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_to_the_partition_of_India "Opposition to the partition of India").[^16] [^17] [^18] However, he eventually migrated to Pakistan in 1956, and decided to live in [Karachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi "Karachi").[^3]

### Communism

In his poems, he supported [communism in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism_in_Pakistan "Socialism in Pakistan").[^19] References to [class consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_consciousness "Class consciousness") are also seen in his poems. He also was described as *"An [anarchist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism "Anarchism"), a [nihilist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihilism "Nihilism"), and a poet"*.[^20]

## Death, legacy and popular culture

Jaun Elia died on 8 November 2002 at age 70 in Karachi, Pakistan.[^21] Some people that knew him called him a difficult, temperamental and complicated person. He also drank alcohol excessively which then contributed to his health problems.[^3] [^22]

As per author Inam Abidi Amrohvi:

> To understand Jaun Elia, one must delve into his poetry and philosophy, which say you must break yourself to build yourself.[^23]

Poet [Pirzada Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirzada_Qasim "Pirzada Qasim") said:

> Jaun was very particular about language. While his diction is rooted in the [classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classicism "Classicism") tradition, he touches on new subjects. He remained in quest of an ideal all his life. Unable to find the ideal eventually, he became angry and frustrated. He felt, perhaps with reason, that he had squandered his talent.[^24]

According to [BBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_News "BBC News") website:

> Jaun Elia's friend Qamar Razi once said of him, "He was quick to criticize, but he was a true friend, a lost traveler in his thoughts".[^3]
> 
> Noted Indian poet [Majrooh Sultanpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majrooh_Sultanpuri "Majrooh Sultanpuri") called him "the poet of poets"...[^3]

In 2020, Punjabi rapper Kay Kap's album *Rough Rhymes for Tough Times* featured a song entitled *Bulaava* which had couplets from the poem *Pehnaayi Ka Makaan* written and recited by Jaun Elia.[^25]

In 2020, Pakistani rock-fusion band Nishtar Park released a single *Purane Aur Naye Sawal* which was based on Elia's ghazal *Umr Guzaregi Imtihan Mein Kya*.

In 2023, Urdu rapper [Talha Anjum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talha_Anjum "Talha Anjum") ’s album *Open Letter* featured a song entitled *Secrets* which was strongly influenced by Jaun Elia’s poem *Be-dilli Kya Yuhin Din Guzar Jaenge*. Talha Anjum’s other works are also heavily influenced by Jaun Elia’s poetry.

### Commemorative postage stamp

On his death anniversary of 8 November 2013, [Pakistan Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Post "Pakistan Post") issued a commemorative postage stamp to honor him for his literary services in its 'Men of Letters' series.[^26]

## Awards and recognition

- [Pride of Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_of_Performance "Pride of Performance") award by the [President of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Pakistan "President of Pakistan") for his literary services in 2000.[^27] [^21]
